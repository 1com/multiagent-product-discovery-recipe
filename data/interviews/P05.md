Interviewer: Tell me about how dinner usually happens in your household during the week.
Participant: I work from home, so I'm in the kitchen at odd hours. There is no routine, I just get hungry and figure it out. Planning ahead stresses me out. A plan I don't follow feels like failure, so I decide on the day.

Interviewer: What's the most frustrating part of all that?
Participant: I buy vegetables with good intentions and then bin them on Sunday. It's a bit embarrassing. Around four in the afternoon the 'what do we eat tonight' dread starts.

Interviewer: Can you walk me through last Tuesday evening?
Participant: Last Tuesday I only started thinking about food at seven, when I was already starving. We had toast with whatever cheese was left.

Interviewer: Tell me about the last time this went wrong.
Participant: I tried a meal-planning app once. By Thursday I hadn't followed the plan and I just stopped opening it.

Interviewer: How do you handle groceries and the food you already have at home?
Participant: I go to the shop almost every day and buy as I go.

Interviewer: What tools or apps, if any, do you use for this today?
Participant: Nothing really, maybe a delivery app now and then.

Interviewer: Who else is involved in deciding what to cook and what to buy?
Participant: Both of us, but not in a coordinated way.

Interviewer: If an app helped with this, what would it need to get right for you to trust it?
Participant: Setup has to be fast. If I need to type in my whole pantry I'll quit on day two. I want to edit the plan easily. I don't want an app dictating to me.

Interviewer: Would you pay for something like that? What would feel fair?
Participant: Honestly I wouldn't pay for it. I'd want a free version.

Interviewer: Is there anything else you would like to add?
Participant: That I'd try it if a friend recommended it, not from an advert.
