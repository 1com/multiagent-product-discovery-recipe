Interviewer: Tell me about how dinner usually happens in your household during the week.
Participant: We cook every day, we've done it for forty years. I know what we like and what is in the cupboard. Our daughter says we should use an app for the shopping list, but we have never needed one. We eat at six sharp and my husband does the dishes. That has not changed in decades.

Interviewer: What's the most frustrating part of all that?
Participant: Not much, honestly. Sometimes I'd like fresh ideas, but it isn't a problem.

Interviewer: Can you walk me through last Tuesday evening?
Participant: On Tuesday we had pork with cabbage, like most Tuesdays. We shop at the same stall every week.

Interviewer: Tell me about the last time this went wrong.
Participant: Not really. Once we bought too much for a family visit, but that was a one-off.

Interviewer: How do you handle groceries and the food you already have at home?
Participant: I go to the shop almost every day and buy as I go.

Interviewer: What tools or apps, if any, do you use for this today?
Participant: A paper pad on the fridge.

Interviewer: Who else is involved in deciding what to cook and what to buy?
Participant: Both of us, but not in a coordinated way.

Interviewer: If an app helped with this, what would it need to get right for you to trust it?
Participant: I don't trust apps with my shopping habits, so it would have to be very simple and private.

Interviewer: Would you pay for something like that? What would feel fair?
Participant: Honestly I wouldn't pay for it. I'd want a free version.

Interviewer: Is there anything else you would like to add?
Participant: No, that covers it. Thanks for the chat.
