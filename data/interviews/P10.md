participant_persona: Four flatmates (late twenties) sharing one kitchen in a city flat
date: 2026-09-14

Interviewer: Tell me about how dinner usually happens in your household during the week.
Participant: Four of us share the kitchen, so mostly everyone cooks for themselves. On weekends we sometimes cook together.

Interviewer: What's the most frustrating part of all that?
Participant: The group chat for groceries is a mess. Somebody always forgets to read it. In the flat the shared stuff gets used up and nobody replaces it. Nobody remembers what we already have, so we buy duplicates and then one of them goes bad.

Interviewer: Can you walk me through last Tuesday evening?
Participant: Last Tuesday three of us cooked at the same time and fought over the two pans. Nobody had bought oil.

Interviewer: Tell me about the last time this went wrong.
Participant: Someone used my eggs and then replaced them with a different size. We now have a labelled shelf, which only half works.

Interviewer: How do you handle groceries and the food you already have at home?
Participant: I glance in the fridge before I leave, but only roughly. I forget half of what's in there.

Interviewer: What tools or apps, if any, do you use for this today?
Participant: We use a shared list app, but it's only a list.

Interviewer: Who else is involved in deciding what to cook and what to buy?
Participant: Everyone in the flat, which is exactly the problem.

Interviewer: If an app helped with this, what would it need to get right for you to trust it?
Participant: Setup has to be fast. If I need to type in my whole pantry I'll quit on day two.

Interviewer: Would you pay for something like that? What would feel fair?
Participant: A few euros a month, maybe, if it clearly saves me money on groceries.

Interviewer: Is there anything else you would like to add?
Participant: Only that I'd love something that just works without being a project.
