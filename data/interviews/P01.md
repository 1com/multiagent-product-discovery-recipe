participant_persona: Mother of two (6 and 9), both parents work full-time, lives in a flat in a mid-sized city
date: 2026-09-02

Interviewer: Tell me about how dinner usually happens in your household during the week.
Participant: It's chaos between school pickups, homework and work calls. Dinner is whatever I can get on the table in thirty minutes.

Interviewer: What's the most frustrating part of all that?
Participant: It's the mental load. Every single day somebody has to decide what's for dinner, and that somebody is me. Things disappear in the back of the fridge and I find them two weeks later, usually when they're already off.

Interviewer: Can you walk me through last Tuesday evening?
Participant: Last Tuesday I picked up the kids at five, we got home at six, and I realised at six that I had nothing planned. I made fish fingers and frozen peas, again.

Interviewer: Tell me about the last time this went wrong.
Participant: Last week I bought a big bag of spinach for a recipe I never made. It went slimy in the drawer and I felt terrible about it.

Interviewer: How do you handle groceries and the food you already have at home?
Participant: I order online every week, mostly the same basket.

Interviewer: What tools or apps, if any, do you use for this today?
Participant: A notes app for the list, and I screenshot recipes that I never cook.

Interviewer: Who else is involved in deciding what to cook and what to buy?
Participant: Mostly me and my partner. The kids have opinions but no responsibility.

Interviewer: If an app helped with this, what would it need to get right for you to trust it?
Participant: Recipes must be realistic for weeknights. Thirty minutes at most. I want to edit the plan easily. I don't want an app dictating to me.

Interviewer: Would you pay for something like that? What would feel fair?
Participant: A few euros a month, maybe, if it clearly saves me money on groceries.

Interviewer: Is there anything else you would like to add?
Participant: Only that I'd love something that just works without being a project.
