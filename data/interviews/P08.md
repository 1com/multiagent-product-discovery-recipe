Interviewer: Tell me about how dinner usually happens in your household during the week.
Participant: I decide around five o'clock. I open the fridge, stare into it for a while, and then improvise something.

Interviewer: What's the most frustrating part of all that?
Participant: Things disappear in the back of the fridge and I find them two weeks later, usually when they're already off. Last summer we went on holiday for three weeks and I forgot about the fridge. I came back to a smell I will never forget and we threw out maybe two hundred euros of food. I swore it would never happen again.

Interviewer: Can you walk me through last Tuesday evening?
Participant: Last Tuesday I was away for work and my partner ate crackers. When I'm home we usually wing it.

Interviewer: Tell me about the last time this went wrong.
Participant: The holiday fridge disaster is the obvious one. Since then I check obsessively before travelling, but I still forget small things.

Interviewer: How do you handle groceries and the food you already have at home?
Participant: I glance in the fridge before I leave, but only roughly. I forget half of what's in there.

Interviewer: What tools or apps, if any, do you use for this today?
Participant: I follow a few recipe accounts for ideas and almost never cook from them.

Interviewer: Who else is involved in deciding what to cook and what to buy?
Participant: Both of us, but not in a coordinated way.

Interviewer: If an app helped with this, what would it need to get right for you to trust it?
Participant: Setup has to be fast. If I need to type in my whole pantry I'll quit on day two.

Interviewer: Would you pay for something like that? What would feel fair?
Participant: For something that would stop that, I'd pay thirty euros a month, easy.

Interviewer: Is there anything else you would like to add?
Participant: Just that every household is different, so it has to flex.
