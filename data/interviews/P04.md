Interviewer: Tell me about how dinner usually happens in your household during the week.
Participant: With three kids there's always somebody with a preference or a food they refuse. Dinner is a negotiation every night.

Interviewer: What's the most frustrating part of all that?
Participant: The worst part is the daily question of what to cook. By Wednesday I have no ideas left. Sharing the shopping list is annoying. I add things, and my wife buys without checking.

Interviewer: Can you walk me through last Tuesday evening?
Participant: Tuesday I made pasta for four, with one separate portion without sauce for the youngest, and a different vegetable because the middle one hates peppers.

Interviewer: Tell me about the last time this went wrong.
Participant: My wife bought chicken and I bought chicken, the same afternoon. We ended up with six breasts and a freezer that was already full.

Interviewer: How do you handle groceries and the food you already have at home?
Participant: I shop once a week, usually on Saturday, plus a couple of top-up trips.

Interviewer: What tools or apps, if any, do you use for this today?
Participant: We use a shared list app, but it's only a list.

Interviewer: Who else is involved in deciding what to cook and what to buy?
Participant: Me, my husband and, loudly, three children.

Interviewer: If an app helped with this, what would it need to get right for you to trust it?
Participant: It would need to understand allergies and what we actually like, otherwise it's just noise. Recipes must be realistic for weeknights. Thirty minutes at most.

Interviewer: Would you pay for something like that? What would feel fair?
Participant: A few euros a month, maybe, if it clearly saves me money on groceries.

Interviewer: Is there anything else you would like to add?
Participant: No, that covers it. Thanks for the chat.
