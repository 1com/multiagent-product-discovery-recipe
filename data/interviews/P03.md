Interviewer: Tell me about how dinner usually happens in your household during the week.
Participant: I cook every night because it's cheaper. I plan on Sunday, otherwise I overspend on the way home. Planning is my favourite part of the week, honestly. I would never want an app to take that away from me.

Interviewer: What's the most frustrating part of all that?
Participant: The waste bothers me, both the money and the guilt. I probably lose twenty or thirty euros a week. Deciding is exhausting. I would rather cook anything than think about what to cook.

Interviewer: Can you walk me through last Tuesday evening?
Participant: On Tuesday I cooked the lentil dish from my Sunday plan, it took forty minutes and my daughter ate half. It's fine, it's what we can afford.

Interviewer: Tell me about the last time this went wrong.
Participant: Once the plan fell apart because a shop was out of an ingredient, and I bought takeaway out of frustration. That cost me thirty euros I didn't have.

Interviewer: How do you handle groceries and the food you already have at home?
Participant: I shop once a week, usually on Saturday, plus a couple of top-up trips.

Interviewer: What tools or apps, if any, do you use for this today?
Participant: A paper pad on the fridge.

Interviewer: Who else is involved in deciding what to cook and what to buy?
Participant: Just me.

Interviewer: If an app helped with this, what would it need to get right for you to trust it?
Participant: It would need to understand allergies and what we actually like, otherwise it's just noise. I want to edit the plan easily. I don't want an app dictating to me.

Interviewer: Would you pay for something like that? What would feel fair?
Participant: Only if it clearly reduced food waste. A free version with ads would also be fine.

Interviewer: Is there anything else you would like to add?
Participant: I think the biggest thing is not making it feel like homework.
