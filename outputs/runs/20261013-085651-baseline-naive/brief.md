# PantryPal discovery brief

## 1. Recommendation
Recommendation: test_further. While households report significant financial loss from food waste and high mental load from daily meal decisions, there is strong resistance to manual inventory entry and rigid meal plans that do not flex with busy schedules. Further validation is needed on low-friction inventory tracking before building. [P01:t2, P02:t8, P05:t4, S04]

## 2. Who the users are
Target users are busy households of two or more people who cook frequently. Evidence identifies three primary segments: working parents managing 'chaos' between work and school [P01:t1, P04:t1], dual-income couples working long hours who struggle with coordination [P02:t1, P08:t1], and shared flatmate households managing common ingredients [P10:t1, P10:t2]. Single students [P07] and retired couples with established routines [P09] do not exhibit the target problems.

## 3. Problems that are real
- **Mental Load of Decision Making:** Users spend significant time deciding what to cook, described as 'the daily question' and 'mental load' [P01:t2, P04:t2]. Adults spend an average of 22 minutes a day on this [S04].
- **Food Waste and Financial Loss:** Households discard roughly 19% of food [S01]. Individual users report losing 'twenty or thirty euros a week' [P03:t2] or finding 'slimy' vegetables in drawers [P01:t4].
- **Coordination Failure:** Multi-person households buy duplicates, such as buying 'basil twice in one week' [P02:t4] or 'six breasts' of chicken when the freezer is full [P04:t4].

## 4. What the evidence says
The evidence consists of 10 interviews (6 of which are high-quality and confirm the problem) and market data. Stated preference indicates a desire for realistic recipes under 30 minutes [P01:t8, P04:t8]. Behavioral data shows users currently rely on 'shared list apps' [P02:t6, P04:t6, P10:t6] or 'paper pads' [P03:t6, P09:t6] but still fail to track inventory. 11% of households already use a meal-planning app, suggesting a niche but established market [S03].

## 5. Competitors and market
The market is crowded with specialized tools. MealLoop targets families with weekly plans [C:MealLoop], while PantryGenie focus on inventory tracking with barcode scanning [C:PantryGenie]. SupperSync addresses the shared household niche with chore assignments [C:SupperSync]. FridgeChef uses photo recognition for recipe suggestions [C:FridgeChef]. Market data shows a median willingness to pay of EUR 3.50 per month [S05], though interviewees range from EUR 0 [P05:t9] to EUR 30 [P08:t9].

## 6. Risks and open assumptions
- **Inventory Friction:** A major risk is that users will abandon the app if data entry is required. Users stated, 'If I need to type in my whole pantry I'll quit on day two' [P02:t8, P05:t8, P10:t8].
- **Plan Rigidity:** Users fear being 'dictated to' [P01:t8, P02:t8, P03:t8]. A previous user of a meal-planning app stopped because they felt like a 'failure' when they didn't follow the plan [P05:t4].
- **Inventory Accuracy:** The assumption that an app can maintain an accurate pantry list without constant user input is untested.

## 7. What to test next
- **Experiment 1 (Low-Friction Inventory):** Test a receipt-scanning or photo-recognition feature to populate inventory. Success metric: % of users who complete setup without manual typing.
- **Experiment 2 (Flexible Planning):** Test a 'choose your own adventure' plan that offers 3 options per night based on expiring items. Success metric: Retention rate at Day 7 compared to a fixed-plan prototype.
- **Experiment 3 (Willingness to Pay):** Offer a EUR 3.50/month subscription on a landing page [S05]. Success metric: Click-through rate on 'Subscribe' button.

## 8. Unsupported or weak claims
- The claim that the meal-kit market is growing at 25% annually lacks a source year and cannot be verified [S06].
- The claim that 73% of families struggle with dinner has no source [S07].
- The claim that 97% of households would pay for automated planning is from a blog and contradicts interview data where several users wanted a free version [S08, P05:t9, P07:t9, P09:t9].
- Evidence from P06 is considered weak due to highly leading questions by the interviewer [P06:t2, P06:t4, P06:t8]."}
