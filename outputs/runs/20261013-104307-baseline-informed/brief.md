# PantryPal discovery brief

## 1. Recommendation
We recommend to test further before committing to build the full PantryPal product [idea_brief]. Research confirms that households experience recurring food waste and dinner decision fatigue [P01:t2, P02:t2, P03:t2, P04:t2, S01, S04]. However, the core concept hinges on households maintaining an active pantry inventory, which users explicitly warn they will abandon if it requires high setup effort [P02:t8, P08:t8, P10:t8]. Furthermore, shopping rhythms dictate planning viability, and willingness to pay remains conditional and unvalidated [P01:t9, P02:t9, P03:t9, P05:t9, P09:t9, S05].

## 2. Who the users are
The idea brief defines the target customer as households of two or more people who cook at home on most weeknights [idea_brief]. Evaluating 10 interviewees against this definition shows that target fit does not automatically imply problem fit [P01, P02, P03, P04, P05, P06, P07, P08, P09, P10]. P06 was excluded from all customer findings due to severe interviewer bias and leading questions [P06:t1]. The remaining 9 usable interviews span three distinct fit categories and shopping rhythms:
- Target segment: Busy families with children (2 of 2 usable target families: P01, P04; raw count 2, evidence-weighted count 1.70). P01 shops weekly online with a fixed basket, while P04 shops weekly with top-up trips [P01:t5, P04:t5]. Both experience dinner as time-pressured management [P01:t1, P04:t1]. P03 is an adjacent single-parent household (1 of 1; weight 0.70) shopping weekly on a tight budget [P03:t1, P03:t5].
- Target segment: Working couples without children (2 of 2 usable target couples: P02, P09; raw count 2, evidence-weighted count 1.80). P02 shops frequently without coordination and cooks pasta or quick meals after late work [P02:t1, P02:t2, P02:t7]. In contrast, P09 is a retired couple cooking daily for 40 years with a fixed routine who do not experience planning problems [P09:t1, P09:t2].
- Adjacent segments: Working couples with irregular schedules or travel (P05, P08; raw count 2, evidence-weighted count 1.00). P05 buys groceries daily and decides meals on the day [P05:t1, P05:t5]. P08 travels frequently for work and improvises dinner [P08:t1, P08:t3]. Both represent partial fits [P05, P08].
- Adjacent segment: Shared flatmates (P10; raw count 1, evidence-weighted count 0.50). Four flatmates cook mostly separately and share communal items with minimal coordination [P10:t1, P10:t2].
- Out-of-target: Single student living alone (P07; raw count 1, evidence-weighted count 0.25). Cooks once a week and relies on takeout, exhibiting no dinner planning struggle [P07:t1, P07:t2].

## 3. Problems that are real
Based on past-behaviour evidence, the following problems are validated:
- Food waste in refrigerators: 4 of 4 usable target households (P01, P02, P03, P04) and 3 of 3 adjacent households (P05, P08, P10) report throwing away food; raw count 7, evidence-weighted count 4.80 [P01:t4, P02:t2, P02:t4, P03:t2, P05:t2, P08:t2, P10:t2]. P01 reported: "Last week I bought a big bag of spinach for a recipe I never made. It went slimy in the drawer and I felt terrible about it." [P01:t4]. P02 reported: "We bought basil twice in one week because each of us thought we were out. Both went bad." [P02:t4]. Across Europe, households discard approximately 19% of the food they buy [S01].
- Mental load of daily meal decisions: 3 of 4 usable target households (P01, P02, P04; raw count 3, evidence-weighted count 2.60) report decision fatigue [P01:t2, P02:t3, P04:t2]. P01 stated: "Every single day somebody has to decide what's for dinner, and that somebody is me." [P01:t2]. Adults spend an average of 22 minutes per day deciding what to cook, based on 2023 data [S04]. However, P03 presents a direct contradiction: P03 stated: "Planning is my favourite part of the week, honestly. I would never want an app to take that away from me." [P03:t1], yet also stated: "Deciding is exhausting. I would rather cook anything than think about what to cook." [P03:t2]. Because of this internal contradiction, P03 is lowered in confidence [P03:t1, P03:t2]. Retired couple P09 does not experience this problem [P09:t2].
- Shopping coordination breakdowns and duplicate purchases: 2 of 4 target households (P02, P04) and 1 adjacent flatshare (P10) experience uncoordinated purchasing leading to waste or duplicates; raw count 3, evidence-weighted count 2.10 [P02:t2, P02:t4, P04:t4, P10:t2]. P04 described: "My wife bought chicken and I bought chicken, the same afternoon. We ended up with six breasts and a freezer that was already full." [P04:t4].

## 4. What the evidence says
- Stated preferences versus past behaviour: While participants express frustration when prompted, their actual past behaviour shows reliance on basic tools [P01:t6, P02:t6, P03:t6, P04:t6, P09:t6]. P01 uses a notes app and recipe screenshots [P01:t6]. P02, P04, and P10 use shared list apps [P02:t6, P04:t6, P10:t6]. P03 and P09 use paper pads on the fridge [P03:t6, P09:t6]. Only 11% of households currently use a meal-planning app [S03].
- Stated requirements: Across target and adjacent segments, participants insist on low effort and flexible control [P01:t8, P02:t8, P03:t8, P04:t8, P05:t8, P08:t8, P10:t8]. Identical templated concerns emerged across interviews: P02, P05, P07, P08, and P10 each warned: "Setup has to be fast. If I need to type in my whole pantry I'll quit on day two." [P02:t8, P05:t8, P07:t8, P08:t8, P10:t8]. P01, P02, P03, and P05 noted: "I want to edit the plan easily. I don't want an app dictating to me." [P01:t8, P02:t8, P03:t8, P05:t8]. P01 and P04 specified that recipes must take thirty minutes at most [P01:t8, P04:t8]. P03 and P04 require allergy and taste filters [P03:t8, P04:t8].
- Willingness to pay (stated intent): Willingness to pay is not validated [P01:t9, P02:t9, P03:t9, P04:t9, P05:t9, P07:t9, P08:t9, P09:t9, P10:t9]. When asked, target users condition payment on verified grocery savings: P01, P04, and P10 said "A few euros a month, maybe, if it clearly saves me money on groceries." [P01:t9, P04:t9, P10:t9]. P02 suggested five or six euros before reverting to a notes app [P02:t9]. P03, P05, P07, and P09 prefer a free or ad-supported version [P03:t9, P05:t9, P07:t9, P09:t9]. An outlier was P08, who stated an intent to pay thirty euros a month, but this was anchored entirely to a single holiday spoilage incident rather than routine meal planning [P08:t2, P08:t9]. The typical stated willingness to pay ranges between EUR 0 and EUR 6 per month [P01:t9, P02:t9, P03:t9, P04:t9, P05:t9, P09:t9].

## 5. Competitors and market
- Competitor landscape: The market contains established tools addressing either inventory tracking or weekly meal planning, contradicting the idea brief claim that existing recipe apps do not know what you own [idea_brief, C:FridgeChef, C:MealLoop, C:PlanPlate, C:PantryGenie, C:WeekWise, C:ZeroWaste Kitchen, C:SupperSync].
  - PantryGenie (EUR 3.99/month, verified 2026-06-18) provides barcode scanning pantry tracking with expiry alerts [C:PantryGenie].
  - MealLoop (EUR 6.99/month or EUR 49.99/year, verified 2026-07-30) targets families with weekly meal plans and automatic grocery lists [C:MealLoop].
  - SupperSync (EUR 4.49/month per household, verified 2026-08-25) coordinates shared plans, grocery lists, and cooking rotas [C:SupperSync].
  - FridgeChef (Free with ads or EUR 4.99/month, verified 2026-08-12) suggests recipes from fridge photos [C:FridgeChef].
  - PlanPlate (EUR 2.99/month, last verified 2024-11-05) offers meal planning calendars, though its data may be outdated [C:PlanPlate].
  - ZeroWaste Kitchen (Free/donations, last verified 2025-01-20) offers leftover recipes, though its data may also be outdated [C:ZeroWaste Kitchen].
  - WeekWise offers budget plans for a EUR 29.99 one-off fee (verified 2026-05-02), but its details are unsourced in the market registry [C:WeekWise].
- Differentiation: True differentiation lies in combining inventory awareness with weekly planning and dynamic shopping lists into a single low-friction workflow, as standalone tools only solve one half [C:MealLoop, C:PantryGenie].
- Market pricing benchmark: Two-person households spend an average of EUR 118 per week on groceries [S02]. Median stated willingness to pay for cooking apps is EUR 3.50 per month [S05]. Competitor monthly pricing spans EUR 2.99 to EUR 6.99 [C:PlanPlate, C:MealLoop]. Any subscription priced above EUR 7.00 per month is completely untested [C:MealLoop, S05].

## 6. Risks and open assumptions
- Riskiest assumption: Households will keep a pantry and fridge inventory up to date in an app [idea_brief, P02:t8, P08:t8, P10:t8]. Users explicitly refuse manual entry homework, and failing to maintain inventory renders inventory-based meal plans useless [P02:t8, P03:t10, P05:t4].
- Format mismatch: A rigid weekly dinner plan may not match household cooking realities [P05:t1, P05:t4, P08:t1]. P05 previously abandoned a meal planning app because an unfollowed plan felt like failure [P05:t4]. Whether households prefer a static weekly plan or same-day recipe suggestions based on current inventory remains an open assumption [P05:t1, P08:t1].
- Shopping rhythm segmentation: Weekly plans tied to shopping lists primarily serve weekly bulk shoppers like P01 and P04, but fail for daily shoppers like P05 and P09 [P01:t5, P04:t5, P05:t5, P09:t5].
- Target customer definition: The assumption that all multi-person households who cook weeknights need dinner planning is unvalidated, as shown by retired couple P09 who cook daily without friction [P09:t1, P09:t2].
- Value proposition framing: The claim that the app will stop household arguments is weakly supported, being reported as dinner friction by only 1 couple (P02), and is better reframed as a shared list and purchasing coordination problem [idea_brief, P02:t3, P04:t2, P10:t2].

## 7. What to test next
1. Low-effort inventory capture test: Test whether users will sustain inventory logging using receipt parsing, photo scanning, or one-click staple selection rather than manual typing [P02:t8]. Build a concierge prototype where 50 target households submit fridge photos or grocery receipts weekly for 3 weeks [UNSUPPORTED]. Success metric: at least 60% of households submit inventory updates for 3 consecutive weeks [UNSUPPORTED].
2. Weekly plan versus daily suggestion prototype: Test whether busy households stick to a pre-set weekly calendar or prefer same-day recipe suggestions based on items nearing expiry [P05:t4, P08:t1]. Run an A/B test with 100 households comparing a 7-day dinner calendar against daily evening recipe alerts [UNSUPPORTED]. Success metric: the daily suggestion variant achieves 25% higher recipe completion rate than the weekly plan variant [UNSUPPORTED].
3. Coordination and shopping list trial: Test whether a shared list preventing duplicate grocery purchases provides immediate tangible value to multi-shopper households [P02:t4, P04:t4]. Deploy a shared list prototype to 40 couples [UNSUPPORTED]. Success metric: 70% reduction in self-reported duplicate grocery purchases over 14 days [UNSUPPORTED].
4. Willingness to pay smoke test: Test conversion on landing pages offering PantryPal at EUR 3.49/month and EUR 4.99/month, benchmarked against competitor pricing and median cooking app willingness to pay [C:PantryGenie, S05]. Drive 500 target family visitors per price tier [UNSUPPORTED]. Success metric: an email signup conversion rate of at least 5% on the EUR 3.49/month tier [UNSUPPORTED].

## 8. Unsupported or weak claims
- The claim that existing recipe apps do not know what you own is contradicted by C:FridgeChef, which offers recipe suggestions from photos of fridge contents, and C:PantryGenie, which tracks pantry inventory [idea_brief, C:FridgeChef, C:PantryGenie].
- The claim that dinner planning makes households argue about what is for dinner is supported as a fight by only one couple (P02:t3) and is unsupported as a universal household driver [idea_brief, P02:t3].
- Market snippet S06 claiming 25% annual market growth is unsupported due to missing source and date [S06].
- Market snippet S07 claiming 73% of families struggle with dinner decisions is unsupported due to missing source and date [S07].
- Market snippet S08 claiming 97% of households would pay for automated meal planning comes from an unreliable blog source and is unsupported [S08].
- Market snippet S09 citing 4% online grocery share is stale data from 2016 and cannot be used as an accurate reflection of current online grocery behaviour [S09].
- Interview P06 is completely excluded due to leading questions and templated copying, providing zero evidential support [P06:t1].
- Willingness to pay of EUR 30/month from P08 is an unrepresentative outlier anchored on holiday food spoilage rather than routine meal planning [P08:t2, P08:t9].
