# PantryPal discovery brief

## 1. Recommendation
PantryPal is recommended for further testing rather than immediate building [idea_brief]. While food waste and decision fatigue are validated pain points, significant usability risks regarding manual pantry upkeep and mixed willingness to pay require validation through prototyping [P02:t8, P05:t8, P07:t8, P08:t8, P10:t8, P05:t9, P09:t9].

## 2. Who the users are
The target customer is defined as households of two or more people who cook at home on most weeknights [idea_brief]. Research spans four distinct segments:
- Families with Children (sample size 3 of 3 interviews: P01, P03, P04; target fit: in): Balancing work, school pickups, and complex dietary preferences; they need quick weeknight recipes under thirty minutes and waste reduction [P01, P03, P04].
- Couples Without Children (sample size 4 of 4 interviews: P02, P05, P08, P09; target fit: mixed): Ranging from busy working professionals with erratic schedules to retired couples; they need coordinated shopping lists and low-effort setup [P02, P05, P08, P09].
- Shared Households / Flatmates (sample size 1 of 1 interview: P10; target fit: partial): Multiple flatmates sharing a single kitchen who need shared visibility of pantry and grocery needs [P10].
- Single Households (sample size 1 of 1 interview: P07; target fit: out): Individuals living alone who predominantly eat out or cook minimal portions; they are out-of-target [P07].

## 3. Problems that are real
- Food waste and forgotten items expiring at the back of the fridge: Strong support from 6 of 9 usable target and adjacent interviews (P01, P02, P03, P05, P08, P10) [P01:t2, P02:t2, P03:t2, P05:t2, P08:t2, P10:t2]. Behavioural and stated evidence show items are lost, noting that the quote "Things disappear in the back of the fridge and I find them two weeks later, usually when they're already off." appears identically as a templated response across P01 and P08 (raw support: 6 interviews; evidence-weighted support is lower due to templating) [P01:t2, P08:t2]. Additional raw quotes include "We throw away so much food. Half a cucumber, wilted herbs, yoghurt that expired last week." [P02:t2]. Frustration questions were prompted [UNSUPPORTED]. Market data shows households discard 19% of purchased food [S01], with average weekly grocery spends of EUR 118 [S02].
- Daily decision fatigue regarding weeknight cooking: Strong support from 6 of 9 usable interviews (P01, P02, P03, P04, P05, P08) [P01:t1, P01:t2, P02:t1, P03:t2, P04:t2, P05:t2]. Stated preference and behaviour evidence show stress, such as "Every single day somebody has to decide what's for dinner, and that somebody is me." [P01:t2] and "Around four in the afternoon the 'what do we eat tonight' dread starts." [P05:t2]. Market evidence indicates adults spend 22 minutes deciding what to cook, with a 3-year age caveat on the data [S04].

## 4. What the evidence says
Evidence rests on 9 usable interviews (P01-P05, P07-P10) [idea_brief]. Interview P06 is excluded entirely due to major leading interview issues and verbatim copying [UNSUPPORTED]. Requirement phrasing regarding setup speed ("Setup has to be fast. If I need to type in my whole pantry I'll quit on day two.") appeared identically across P02, P05, P07, P08, and P10, indicating potential interviewer probing bias [P02:t8, P05:t8, P07:t8, P08:t8, P10:t8].
Contradictions exist regarding meal planning and automation:
- Structured planning vs. spontaneous cooking: Some users enjoy planning ("Planning is my favourite part of the week, honestly. I would never want an app to take that away from me." [P03:t1]), while others find it stressful ("Planning ahead stresses me out. A plan I don't follow feels like failure, so I decide on the day." [P05:t1]).
- Trust in automation: Some users want editing control ("I want to edit the plan easily. I don't want an app dictating to me." [P01:t8]), while others distrust automated apps entirely ("I don't trust apps with my shopping habits, so it would have to be very simple and private." [P09:t8]).
Willingness to pay typically ranges from a few euros up to 5-6 euros per month, conditional on saving money or reducing waste [P01:t9, P02:t9, P04:t9, P10:t9]. Participants P05 and P09 state they would not pay and require a free ad-supported version [P05:t9, P09:t9]. An outlier (P08) stated 30 euros per month, which is excluded from typical figures as it was anchored on a catastrophic holiday fridge disaster [P08:t9]. Market benchmark S05 is EUR 3.50 per month [S05]. All willingness to pay figures represent stated intent [UNSUPPORTED].

## 5. Competitors and market
Competitor analysis reveals several active and stale alternatives:
- C:FridgeChef (verified 2026-08-12, current): tracks inventory via photos/recipes; Free with ads, Premium EUR 4.99/month [C:FridgeChef].
- C:MealLoop (verified 2026-07-30, current): weekly plan, shopping list, family sharing; EUR 6.99/month or EUR 49.99/year [C:MealLoop].
- C:PlanPlate (verified 2004-11-05, stale): weekly plan, sharing; EUR 2.99/month [C:PlanPlate].
- C:PantryGenie (verified 2026-06-18, current): inventory and expiry alerts; EUR 3.99/month [C:PantryGenie].
- C:WeekWise (verified 2026-05-02 with unsourced URL, current): budget plans; Free; EUR 29.99 one-off [C:WeekWise].
- C:ZeroWaste Kitchen (verified 2025-01-20, stale): leftover recipes; Free [C:ZeroWaste Kitchen].
- C:SupperSync (verified 2026-08-25, current): shared household planner; EUR 4.49/month [C:SupperSync].
The idea-brief claim that existing recipe apps do not know what you already own is contradicted by C:FridgeChef and C:PantryGenie [C:FridgeChef, C:PantryGenie]. True differentiation requires combining inventory awareness with weekly planning [UNSUPPORTED]. Meal-planning app market penetration is 11% [S03].

## 6. Risks and open assumptions
- Usability risk: Households will keep a pantry inventory up to date manually or via low-effort input [P02:t8, P05:t8, P07:t8, P08:t8, P10:t8]. Confidence is low; impact if wrong is high, as users explicitly stated they would quit if typing is required [P02:t8, P05:t8, P07:t8, P08:t8, P10:t8].
- Desirability risk: Households want automated weekly dinner plans combined with inventory tracking [P01:t2, P02:t1, P03:t2, P04:t2, P05:t2]. Confidence is medium [UNSUPPORTED].
- Viability risk: Users will pay a monthly subscription fee [P01:t9, P02:t9, P04:t9, P10:t9]. Confidence is low due to demands for free ad-supported versions [P05:t9, P09:t9].
- Desirability risk: PantryPal can solve household shopping coordination and reduce food waste [P01:t2, P02:t2, P03:t2, P05:t2, P08:t2, P10:t2]. Confidence is medium [UNSUPPORTED].
The target definition itself remains an assumption to test [idea_brief].

## 7. What to test next
Experiments are prioritized as follows:
1. Test inventory input effort: Ask 20 target households to test a prototype receipt or photo scanner interface for one week and track daily inventory updates [UNSUPPORTED]. Success metric: At least 70 percent of participants log inventory and return to update it at least twice during the week [UNSUPPORTED].
2. Test willingness to pay: Run a landing page test presenting free versus premium pricing tiers to 500 targeted visitors [UNSUPPORTED]. Success metric: At least 5 percent click through and enter payment details [UNSUPPORTED].
Open questions to explore include comparing weekly plans against same-day 'what can I cook tonight' suggestions, evaluating shopping rhythm segments (weekly vs. daily shoppers) [P03:t5, P04:t5, P05:t5, P09:t5], determining optimal inventory input methods, and tracking long-term usage [UNSUPPORTED].

## 8. Unsupported or weak claims

- WeekWise competitor details rely on an unsourced URL [C:WeekWise].
- P08's willingness to pay of EUR 30/month is an unvalidated outlier anchored on a catastrophic holiday fridge incident [P08:t9].
