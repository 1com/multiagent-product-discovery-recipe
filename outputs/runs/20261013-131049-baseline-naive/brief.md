# PantryPal discovery brief

## 1. Recommendation
Recommendation: **Pivot** from an automated planner with AI recipe suggestions to a simple, low-friction inventory tracker that helps users draft their own plans manually.
Why: While there is clear demand for reducing waste and organizing dinners, the specific value proposition of "automatic" planning is rejected by 7 out of 10 interviewees who cited setup difficulty as an immediate quit point [P01:t8, P02:t8, P05:t8, P07:t8, P08:t8, P09:t8, P10:t8]. Competitors with manual elements (like PantryGenie) have smaller but defined audiences, while fully automated tools often lack the friction tolerance that busy households demonstrate.

## 2. Who the users are
The most likely users are:
- **Families (3+ people)**: Struggling with coordination, conflicting preferences, and duplicate buying [P04:t1, P04:t4]. They explicitly pay for "shared lists" but dislike uncoordinated systems [P04:t7].
- **Busy couples without children**: Facing time pressure, food waste due to forgotten items, and arguments over dinner decisions [P02:t3, P08:t2]. They value speed above variety [P02:t1, P08:t1].
- **Single parents / Budget-conscious**: Motivated by waste guilt and cost saving, but often overwhelmed by decision fatigue rather than coordination needs [P03:t1, P03:t9].
- **Irregular households (travelers, freelancers)**: Prone to hoarding during absences and wasting food upon return; they are highly sensitive to setup time [P05:t2, P08:t2].
- **Excluded users**: Students (P07) who cook rarely or rely on takeout do not see value, and long-time cooks with established routines (P09) have little need for external tools [P07:t1, P09:t1].

## 3. Problems that are real
- **Food waste**: Households throw away roughly a fifth of food; participants frequently report wasting specific items like spinach or vegetables due to forgetfulness [P01:t4, S01].
- **Coordination failure**: Duplicate purchases (e.g., buying chicken twice) occur because shopping happens in uncoordinated bursts [P02:t6, P04:t4].
- **Decision fatigue**: Adults spend 22 minutes per day deciding what to cook; for larger families, this becomes a negotiation or argument [S04, P04:t1].
- **Lack of awareness**: Users often forget half the contents of their fridge before shopping [P02:t5, P08:t5].

## 4. What the evidence says
### Stated Preferences vs. Behavior
- **Friction Sensitivity**: Participants repeatedly stated they would quit if setup required typing the whole pantry [P01:t8, P02:t8]. However, actual behavior is fragmented; many use shared list apps but lack inventory depth [P02:t6, P04:t6].
- **Automation Rejection**: Users prefer manual control. One participant said planning was her "favourite part" and she would "never want an app to take that away from me" [P03:t1]. Another said automatic plans feel like "noise" without allergy/ preference context [P03:t8, P04:t8].
- **Willingness to Pay**: Stated willingness to pay ranges widely (EUR 2–30/month) [P02:t9, P05:t9]. However, only those who felt they lost significant money on waste or overspend expressed willingness to pay [P03:t4, P08:t9]. Most others demanded a free version [P05:t9, P07:t9].

### Evidence Strength
- **High strength**: Clear behavioral patterns of waste and duplicate buying across multiple demographics [P01:t4, P02:t6, P04:t4].
- **Medium strength**: Strong stated preference for manual control contradicts the "one-click automated" pitch in the idea brief.
- **Low strength**: Willingness to pay is hypothetical and depends heavily on pain point perception; actual conversion rates are unknown.

## 5. Competitors and market
- **MealLoop (C:MealLoop)** targets families with weekly plans but costs EUR 6.99/month, which most interviewees would reject [S05, P02:t9].
- **PantryGenie (C:PantryGenie)** offers inventory tracking and expiry alerts for EUR 3.99/month; this aligns better with the "reduce waste" motivation but lacks meal planning features.
- **SupperSync (C:SupperSync)** handles chore assignment and shared lists for EUR 4.49/month, addressing the coordination problem but not inventory.
- **Market**: 73% of families struggle with dinner decisions, yet only 11% use a meal-planning app [S07, S03].
- **Gap**: There is no dominant competitor that combines robust inventory management with low-friction manual meal drafting and cost/waste tracking.

## 6. Risks and open assumptions
- **Automation Assumption Risk**: The idea assumes users want "automated" plans to save time. Evidence suggests they want "assisted" planning to reduce decision fatigue without losing control [P03:t1].
- **Setup Friction**: Assuming users can tolerate onboarding is risky; all major interviewees cited fast setup as a critical success factor, with many threatening abandonment if typing the pantry was required [P05:t8, P07:t8].
- **Value Perception**: The value of knowing what's in the fridge may be undervalued by users unless tied to direct financial impact (waste savings).
- **Competitive Pressure**: Existing tools like MealLoop and SupperSync are well-funded; PantryPal needs a unique angle on inventory + manual planning.

3. **Experiment: "Shared Inventory" vs. "Meal Planner"**
   - Success metric: Daily active usage of shared list features over planning features.
   - Sample size: N=40 households (3+ people).
   - Threshold: ≥40% weekly engagement with inventory updates.

## 8. Unsupported or weak claims
- **Claim**: "Users want automated plans."**Evidence:** Mixed; some say yes if fast, but many explicitly reject automation as it removes agency and requires excessive setup time [P03:t1, P05:t8].
- **Claim**: "Users will pay EUR 3–6/month consistently.**Evidence:** Hypothetical only; few trials occurred before pricing discussion, and willingness to pay drops when cost exceeds EUR 5 for non-automatic plans [P02:t9, P03:t9].
- **Claim**: "Students and retirees are not target users."**Evidence:** Partial; students cook rarely (low relevance), but retirees have rigid routines and may resist tech adoption [P07:t1, P09:t1].
